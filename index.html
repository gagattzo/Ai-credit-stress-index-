READ

How to read the index

  1. It tracks the stressed edge of AI credit.The debt side covers the borrowers where AI credit stress shows first: CoreWeave, Oracle and the data-center campuses Oracle leases. Hyperscaler corporate debt (Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Meta) is out of scope.
  2. Default protection also prices hedging.Oracle CDS is widely bought as a hedge against an AI downturn, so it carries hedging demand as well as default risk.
  3. Rents are not the loans' collateral.Construction loans are secured on campuses and leases. GPU rents measure what the hardware earns, not what a lender would recover.
  4. The main judgment calls are tested.Anchors are frozen in method v0.1 and change only with a new method version. The sensitivity table re-runs the gap with each call changed: the 80% utilization behind the GPU anchors, the choice of GPU index, and the two inputs at their limits (CoreWeave past the distress line, Blackwell rents rising).
  5. Live means the CDS inputs.ACSI Live re-prices Oracle and CoreWeave 5-year CDS with every trade DTCC publishes. CoreWeave CDS trades on upfront terms only, so its spread is computed from the upfront with the standard model (flat hazard, 40% recovery). Loans and GPU rents move with each weekly edition.
  6. No ceiling since 27 Nov.Method v0.2: an input scores 0 at its calm level and 100 at its distress line, and keeps rising past it. Under v0.1 it stopped at 100, so CoreWeave, past its line since mid-October, could no longer move the index. The 24 Nov close (+42.6) was v0.1 and stays as published.
  7. History is reconstructed; the official series is not.The flip chart rebuilds earlier months from dated prints with a subset of inputs. The official weekly series starts on 24 Nov 2026 and is never back-filled.

Formula

Each input x gets a score s = 100 × (x − calm) / (distress − calm), clipped to 0–100. The same formula works when lower is worse (loan prices, rents), because then the distress anchor sits below the calm one.

Financing stress is the plain average of the live debt scores. GPU asset stress is the plain average of the live GPU scores. The two are never blended into one number, since averaging would erase the very divergence the index exists to show. Stress gap = financing − asset.

Bands: 0–24 Calm, 25–49 Elevated, 50–74 Stressed, 75–100 Distressed, assigned from unrounded values. A score of 100 is always shown with how far past the distress line the input is.

Anchors and staleness windows

Both spread inputs share one distress line, 1,000 bp, the standard definition of distressed debt: CoreWeave's bond spread over Treasuries and Oracle's CDS premium. Loans run from 98 (calm) to 80, the leveraged-loan market's distressed borderline. H100 anchors are widely cited rental thresholds for roughly a 10% return and for losing money, divided by an assumed 80% utilization. The GPU inputs use Silicon Data's indices, which are steadier and have the longer public history; Ornn's transaction-based index is the cross-check, and the sensitivity table shows the gap on Ornn.

Evidence grades: A, a public transaction record; B, a licensed or index-provider value; C, a named outlet reporting a trade or quote; D, a named outlet reporting an expected level or a one-sided bound. Older than its window, an input is excluded and the others are re-averaged. Financing needs at least two of its three inputs live; GPU stress needs the H100 input live. Nothing is filled in, and a missing loan print is never assumed to be at par. This preview edition uses data to Tuesday 24 Nov; regular editions will use Friday closes.

Rules that keep it honest

  • Anchors are frozen for 52 weekly readings. Any change to inputs, anchors, windows or bands needs a new method version, with old and new published side by side for four weeks.
  • No trading-signal claims before 52 live weeks.
  • Every judgment call is in the sensitivity table, with its effect on the gap.
  • Published values are never silently revised; corrections are dated and show old and new.
