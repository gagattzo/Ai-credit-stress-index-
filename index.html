RISK

What we don't know

10 open

The weak points a credit PM would raise, stated up front.

  1. These are the weakest links, picked with hindsight.The debt inputs were chosen in November 2026 because they were already under strain. This is not an average of AI credit: Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon and Meta corporate debt is out of scope.
  2. Financing is partly an Oracle index.Two of the three debt inputs are Oracle: its own default protection, and loans on campuses Oracle leases.
  3. Loan prices are press-reported quotes and expectations, not trade records."Below mid-80s" and "over 90" are words, taken at the bound. Healthy loans near par never make the news, so this input leans toward stress. SocGen's single "over 90" quote is counted for two loans, which lowers the score; the sensitivity table shows it counted once.
  4. The utilization assumption is the biggest judgment call.GPU anchors assume GPUs are rented 80% of the time. Other choices move the gap a lot; the sensitivity table shows by how much. Changing one call at a time, the gap stays above the aligned band in every case tested, but 70% utilization together with no Blackwell input brings it to about zero.
  5. Rents are not the loans' collateral.Construction loans are secured on campuses and leases. GPU rents measure what the hardware earns, not what a lender would recover.
  6. GPU price indices disagree.Ornn and Silicon Data are close on H100 today but have diverged sharply at times this autumn, and still differ widely on Blackwell. This index uses Silicon Data; Ornn would lower the gap (sensitivity table).
  7. Some of the data is licensed.This preview uses levels reported by named outlets and public index pages. A public launch needs redistribution permission for CDS and GPU index values, including the charts.
  8. No history yet.There is no back-filled index line, because that would claim data that didn't exist. The weekly series starts with the first live edition; the charts show raw prints only.
  9. Default protection also prices hedging.Oracle CDS is widely bought as a hedge against an AI downturn, so it is not a pure measure of default risk.
  10. Two inputs are at their limits.CoreWeave is past the distress line, so it scores 100 however far it widens; the Blackwell trend is rising, so it scores 0 however fast. Leave both out and the gap falls into the aligned band.

Formula

Each input x gets a score s = 100 × (x − calm) / (distress − calm), clipped to 0–100. The same formula works when lower is worse (loan prices, rents), because then the distress anchor sits below the calm one.

Financing stress is the plain average of the live debt scores. GPU asset stress is the plain average of the live GPU scores. The two are never blended into one number, since averaging would erase the very divergence the index exists to show. Stress gap = financing − asset.

Bands: 0–24 Calm, 25–49 Elevated, 50–74 Stressed, 75–100 Distressed, assigned from unrounded values. A score of 100 is always shown with how far past the distress line the input is.

Anchors and staleness windows

Both spread inputs share one distress line, 1,000 bp, the standard definition of distressed debt: CoreWeave's bond spread over Treasuries and Oracle's CDS premium. Loans run from 98 (calm) to 80, the leveraged-loan market's distressed borderline. H100 anchors are widely cited rental thresholds for roughly a 10% return and for losing money, divided by an assumed 80% utilization. The GPU inputs use Silicon Data's indices, which are steadier and have the longer public history; Ornn's transaction-based index is the cross-check, and the sensitivity table shows the gap on Ornn.

Rules that keep it honest

  • Anchors are frozen for 52 weekly readings. Any change to inputs, anchors, windows or bands needs a new method version, with old and new published side by side for four weeks.
  • No trading-signal claims before 52 live weeks.
  • Every judgment call is in the sensitivity table, with its effect on the gap.
  • Published values are never silently revised; corrections are dated and show old and new.

Levels come from press reports and public index pages; each one links to its source. Not investment advice.
